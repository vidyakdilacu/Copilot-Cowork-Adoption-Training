Guide

Before you run your first lab

Read this once. It covers what Cowork can reach, what you need, and how to write a prompt that works.

Scope and boundary

All labs run inside your Microsoft 365 tenant. Cowork works only with content you already have permission to see.

In scope

  • Outlook mail and calendar
  • Teams chats, channels, meeting recaps and transcripts
  • OneDrive and SharePoint files
  • Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Loop
  • Notification emails from other systems in your inbox

Not in this pilot

  • ServiceNow, Salesforce, and other third-party plugins (not yet approved)
  • Approving or updating records in those systems
  • Sending or posting anything without your review
  • Legal conclusions, tax positions, or security sign-off
Working with systems outside Microsoft 365
  • Use the notification emails those systems send to your inbox as the source.
  • Export a report to Excel in OneDrive yourself, then point Cowork at that file.
  • Take the final action in the external system yourself, using Cowork's summary.

What you need

  1. A Microsoft 365 Copilot license with Copilot Cowork turned on. If you can't find Cowork, contact the pilot team.
  2. Real content: a week of email, a few Teams meetings with transcription or recap on, and files in OneDrive or SharePoint.
  3. A OneDrive folder called Cowork Labs for your outputs.
  4. 20–45 minutes per lab. Multi-step tasks can take a few minutes to run.

How the waves work

Wave 1 Start here. Low risk, quick to check, useful in every role.

Wave 2 Move on once you trust the output. These take more steps or change more.

Specialist review First drafts in regulated areas. A qualified reviewer must check them.

Anatomy of a Cowork prompt

Cowork does best with a full brief rather than a question. Every lab prompt has these five parts.

PartWhat it saysExample
GoalThe outcome you want, in one or two sentences.Create my weekly status report for the past 7 days.
SourcesWhere Cowork should look, and where it should not.My meetings, sent mail, Teams chats, and files I edited this week.
StepsThe work broken into ordered steps.Group by project, then list done, in progress, risks, and next steps.
OutputFile type, name, location, length, and audience.A Word doc in OneDrive / Cowork Labs, one page.
CheckpointWhat Cowork must not do without you, and how to show its sources.Draft the email. Do not send. List the sources for each section.

Ground rules

Drafts, not decisions

Every lab ends with a draft. You review, edit, and decide what gets sent or shared.

Ask for sources

Have Cowork list where each point came from, and spot-check at least two.

Your access, your call

Cowork sees what you can see. Check for confidential content before sharing.

Iterate in place

If the result is off, give feedback in the same task rather than starting over.

Help

Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely causeWhat to try
Cowork can't find a meetingNo transcript or recap was recorded, or you weren't an attendee.Turn on transcription for future meetings. Name the meeting and date in the prompt.
A file is missing from the outputYou don't have access, or it's stored outside Microsoft 365.Paste the file link into the prompt, or move a copy to OneDrive.
Output is too long or genericThe prompt has no length limit or audience.Add "Keep it under 200 words for [audience]" and describe the tone.
Facts are wrong or inventedThe source was ambiguous or thin.Ask for sources on each point, and for "Not found" instead of guesses.
Task needs ServiceNow or SalesforceThose plugins aren't approved yet.Use notification emails or an Excel export in OneDrive.
Cowork wants to send or change somethingThe prompt didn't set a checkpoint.Decline, then add "Draft only. Do not send, share, or change anything without my confirmation."
