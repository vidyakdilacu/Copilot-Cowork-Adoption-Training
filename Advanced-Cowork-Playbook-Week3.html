Go further: from a simple task to real delegation

You don't need longer prompts. You need clearer outcomes and better context. Here's one everyday example, step by step.

1 · Start here"Summarize the emails about the Contoso renewal."
2 · Add context"…from the last 2 weeks, including the pricing file and Tuesday's call. I'm meeting their procurement lead Thursday."
3 · Delegate more"Prepare me for Thursday: what's agreed, what's open, likely objections, and 3 options on pricing with risks. Word brief, sourced."
4 · ReviewCheck citations and numbers. Decide which option you back. Remove anything internal-only.
5 · Follow up"Argue against option 2." → "Draft my opening talking points." → "Save this as my 'customer prep' skill."

Contoso is an example name. Use your own project, partner or customer.

1 · Persona work profile: how this cohort works

This is a mixed cohort, so the profile describes shared work first and role differences second.

Shared work across all roles
  • Frequent and recurring: inbox catch-up, weekly status updates, follow-ups, recurring meetings
  • Time-consuming and multi-source: pulling information from email, Teams, files and the web to answer "what's the latest?" or "what should we do?"
  • Recurring deliverables: briefs, status updates, recap emails, proposals, enablement content
  • Meeting-related: preparing for and following up on customer, partner and project meetings
  • Communication-heavy: chasing owners, confirming decisions, summarizing for leadership
  • Already in Cowork: research and summaries, email reading and drafting, role-specific tasks, lots of varied one-offs
  • Could delegate better: the steps after the summary, like comparing, deciding, drafting and following up

3 · Level Up / Connect / Discover opportunities

LEVEL UP
  • Summarize this doc → Analyze these materials and find what matters → Prepare me for the decision (UC1)
  • Summarize this thread → Triage my project email and draft replies (UC2)
  • Explain this error → Build the escalation case (UC5)
  • Write a doc → Draft, critique as the reader, revise (UC6)
CONNECT
  • Meetings + email + chats → status update + follow-ups (UC3)
  • Research + analysis + Word brief in one delegation (UC1)
  • Email triage + reply drafts + action checklist (UC2)
DISCOVER
  • Meeting brief → recap → follow-up loop: meetings aren't a named usage category yet (UC4)
  • Turn repeat one-offs into a personal skill, aimed at the 30% "Other / general assistance" bucket (UC7)
  • Schedule your best pattern: Monday triage, Friday status

7 · Facilitator talking points

1 · Your Cowork patterns (4 min)

You're past the basics. Today is about getting more out of the work you already bring to Cowork.

Research and email are your strongest habits. We'll take both from "summarize" to "prepare me" and "tell me what needs me".

Documents and communication are low, but that's where most research and email ends up. We'll connect them.

"Other / general assistance" is 30%. Some of that is probably repeat work, and it's worth turning into a pattern.

Show bars only, not detailed telemetry. Emphasize that this is a signal, not a scorecard.

2 · Level up (6 min)

Same task, three ways. Watch what changes: not the length of the prompt, but the outcome, context and output I ask for.

Run the Packet 2 ladder on a demo mailbox: Basic → Better → Advanced. Pause on the triage table, and point out "drafts only".

The advanced version did in one pass what used to take me four prompts.

3 · Recommended for you (3 min)

Three scenarios everyone can use this week: decision brief, email-to-action, and weekly status pack.

Click the role chips to show the extra scenarios for each role (escalation summary for technical roles, meeting loop for sales and PM).

4 · Live advanced scenario (9 min)

Run Packet 1 Go Further on a prepared topic.

Context: here's the decision, the audience and the deadline. Delegate: I'm asking for the outcome, not a summary.

Review: I'm checking 3 citations and one number. This one rests on a single source, so I'll treat it carefully.

Follow up: "Argue against your recommendation." That's how you stress-test it.

Use: I'll turn the open questions into a draft email. Draft only.

5–6 · Choose and try (14 min)

Pick the packet closest to a real task on your plate this week. Start with Go Further.

No real data handy? Use a representative task, like a past project or a public topic.

Keep time: 3 minutes to upgrade a prompt, 6 to run a packet, 3 to challenge the result.

Circulate. Common fixes: no outcome stated, no timeframe, asking for "a summary" instead of a decision or output.
